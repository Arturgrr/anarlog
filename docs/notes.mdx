Every meeting note has up to three views:

- **Memos** are the notes you write yourself.
- **Summary** is the structured note generated by your Intelligence model.
- **Transcript** is the captured conversation.

You can edit memos and summaries like regular notes. In a transcript, select a speaker label to assign a participant and use **Apply to all** when the same speaker appears again.

When CLI, MCP, or Chat stages a memo or summary replacement, the meeting shows a **pending edit** banner. Open **Review memo** or **Review summary** to inspect the unified diff, then apply or decline it. A proposal does not change the meeting until you apply it.

After applying a memo or summary edit, use the editor's **Undo** command to restore the previous content. Your earlier typing remains in the undo history.

## Correct a transcript

- After recording stops, click **Write** above the transcript to edit its text. Save with Command/Ctrl+S and press Escape to return to reading.
- Select words or a transcript segment and choose **Change speaker** to fix only that selection. In write mode, press Enter at the cursor to open the speaker picker; selected text can become its own speaker block.
- Select transcript blocks and delete them together. Adjacent blocks assigned to the same speaker merge.
- Select a speaker label to choose an existing participant or **Create new speaker**. Use **Apply to all** to update every matching speaker segment.
- Right-click **Transcript** to copy the transcript, resume listening, re-transcribe retained audio, or delete the recording.

<Warning>
  Re-transcribing permanently replaces the current transcript with output from your selected Transcription model. Edit or export anything you need before you start it.
</Warning>

Deleting the recording does not delete the memo, summary, or transcript. It only removes the retained audio file.

## Speakers in a transcript

Librelog records your microphone and your computer's audio as separate channels, so your own words and everyone you hear through the computer are always told apart.

- **Live on-device models** (Parakeet Streaming, Apple Speech) put everyone heard through the computer under one speaker while the meeting runs. When the meeting ends and the recording is kept, Librelog separates those voices on this Mac. See [Speakers after a live on-device meeting](/models-and-providers#speakers-after-a-live-on-device-meeting) for what it needs.
- **After-recording models**, and hosted providers that support it, separate speakers as part of transcription.
- With exactly one other participant listed, the far end stays one speaker named after that person.
- Your microphone stays one speaker unless the meeting was in person, with no computer audio and its participants listed, and was transcribed after recording.

Separated voices appear as numbered speakers until you name them. Select a speaker label, choose the person, and keep **Apply to all** on to name every segment of that voice. The person is added to the meeting's participants.

With **Settings → Meetings → Remember speakers** on, naming a voice this way also keeps a voiceprint of it on this computer. In later meetings Librelog names that voice for you:

- When the meeting lists two or more other participants, it matches voices against them. When every voice but one is named and the counts match, the last voice gets the remaining name.
- When the meeting lists no participants, it matches voices against everyone you have named before. A voice is named only when it clearly matches one person and nobody else, and that person is not added to the participants.
- When exactly one other participant is listed, the far end is named from the participant list, not from a voiceprint.
- When nothing matches clearly, the voice keeps its number for you to name.

Correcting a selection with **Change speaker** fixes those words only and does not teach Librelog a voice. Voiceprints need the recording until the meeting is processed, so **Don't save** under **Audio file retention** also stops new voiceprints.

### Assign every speaker at once

After the meeting, open the **•••** menu in the note header and choose **Assign speakers…**. The dialog lists one row per speaker, with their word count and a sample of what they said. When the note has several recordings, each row shows which one it belongs to.

1. Click **Choose person** on a row, or the name already shown, and pick a participant or contact.
2. Repeat for each speaker you want to change.
3. Click **Apply**.

Only the rows you changed are written. If Librelog split one person's voice into two speakers, choose the same person for both rows to bring them together. Your own microphone row shows **You** and cannot be changed here.

Naming the last unnamed speaker, here or in the transcript, can start an [automation](/automations#run-a-workflow-when-speakers-are-named).

## Group notes by type

Note types let you group notes, such as a class and its subject or a client and a recurring meeting. Each type can have subtypes, one level deep.

- Manage types in **Settings → Note types**. Add a type, add subtypes under it, and rename or delete either.
- Set a note's type from **Type** when you create the note, or from **Meeting info** in the note's **•••** menu. **Subtype** appears after you choose a type.
- To rename a note and set its type in one place, right-click it in the sidebar and choose **Rename and Set Type…**. This also works while the note is recording.
- Type a new name in the picker and choose **Create "…"** to add a type or subtype without opening Settings.

Deleting a type also deletes its subtypes. Notes that used them show no type; their other content is unchanged.

Automations can run only for one type or subtype, for example to export each type to its own folder or summarize it with its own template. See [Run a workflow for one type of note](/automations#run-a-workflow-for-one-type-of-note).

## Shape the summary

Use the template picker beside **Summary** to change its structure. You can also set standing instructions, add names and specialized terms, and control how templates interact with your preferences.

See [Customize meeting summaries](/customize-summaries) for the complete setup.

## Continue an existing note

Right-click **Transcript** and choose **Resume listening** to add another recording to the same meeting note. This is useful when a call resumes after a break or when you want several related conversations in one place.

## Review your conversation activity

In the desktop app, open **Settings → Insights** to see your busiest weekdays, typical conversation length, and conversations per active day. Choose the last 7 days, last 30 days, or all time. Weekday patterns appear after at least five captured conversations in the selected range.

The same **Insights** page includes your activity over the past year and your weekly streak.

Insights is a desktop view. The CLI and local MCP server do not expose it; use their existing meeting and transcript commands to work with your conversations.

## Export a meeting

1. Open the **•••** menu in the meeting header.
2. Choose **Export**.
3. Select PDF, text, Markdown, or Org format.
4. Include any combination of the memo, summary, and transcript.

Librelog writes the export to your default export folder and reveals it when ready. Change the destination under **Settings → General**.
