---
title: "Dictate into other apps"
description: "Use a keyboard shortcut to turn speech into text in the focused field."
---

Keep Librelog running, focus a text field in another app, and use a dictation shortcut to speak instead of type. This does not create a meeting note.

## Set up dictation

1. Open **Settings → Dictation** and enable dictation.
2. Click a shortcut to change it, or click **Add shortcut**. Press the keys you want, then press Enter to save. You can keep up to four shortcuts, and any of them starts dictation. Then choose a microphone.
3. Use **Language & transcription** to choose the language and Transcription provider. Hosted providers receive your audio; a supported local provider keeps transcription on your computer.
4. Allow microphone access and any permissions requested under **Settings → Permissions**. On macOS, text insertion needs Accessibility permission.
5. Click the **Try dictation** field in Settings and test the shortcut.

Hold the shortcut while speaking and release it to finish. With **Hands-free dictation** enabled, press once to start and again to finish. Text is inserted when transcription finishes. **Live transcript preview** shows words under the dictation indicator at the top of the screen when the selected provider supports a live connection.

You hear a short sound when dictation starts and another when it stops and sends your audio to transcription. Cancelling is silent, and no sound plays while Librelog records a meeting. To dictate silently, turn off **Dictation sounds** in **Settings → Dictation**.

Dictation stops after five minutes. Press Escape to cancel; on Wayland, use Control + Alt + Escape.

Dictation also works while Librelog records a meeting, for example to type into the call's chat. The meeting keeps recording, and what you dictate is part of its microphone audio and transcript. With a self-hosted transcription server, the dictation skips the live preview and is transcribed when you finish, so it doesn't take the meeting's connection. With an on-device model, a dictation may wait a moment while the meeting's audio is being transcribed.

## Where the text goes

On macOS, text goes to whatever has keyboard focus when transcription finishes, so you can move to another field or app while Librelog transcribes.

- Native macOS text fields, such as those in Notes and TextEdit, receive the text directly and your clipboard is left alone.
- Web pages, browsers, Electron apps such as VS Code, Cursor, Slack, Discord, Notion and Obsidian, and terminals such as Terminal, iTerm2, Ghostty and Warp receive the text as a paste. So does any other app whose field doesn't take text directly. Librelog restores your previous clipboard shortly after the app reads it, and marks the dictation so clipboard managers that follow the nspasteboard.org convention skip it.
- Chromium browsers at version 155 or 156, such as Chrome, Edge and Brave, receive the text as typed keystrokes, because those versions can drop a paste.

The paste works with any keyboard layout, including Dvorak, Dvorak – QWERTY ⌘, AZERTY and Cyrillic layouts. Librelog waits up to two seconds for you to release the shortcut keys before it inserts text.

Dictation does not insert into password fields. On macOS it is also unavailable while the focused app has Secure Keyboard Entry on, which Terminal and iTerm2 offer in their app menus.

## If insertion fails

If nothing editable had focus, or the app could not insert the text, open **Settings → Dictation** and choose **Copy last dictation**, then paste it yourself. The last transcript stays in memory until you disable dictation or quit Librelog.

On Windows and Linux, keep the original text field focused until dictation finishes. On Linux, approve the desktop's shortcut prompt on Wayland. The target app must expose an editable accessibility field; password fields are excluded. Clear a selected text range before dictating, or use the copy action to replace it yourself.

Dictation is a desktop interaction. The CLI and MCP interfaces do not start dictation or insert text into other apps.

## Dictation history

**Settings → Dictation history** lists your dictations, newest first: when you dictated, the app the text went to, the model that transcribed it, and the text before and after any cleanup. A dictation whose text could not be inserted is marked **Not inserted**, so you can copy it from there. Search matches the text, the app and the model. The history stays on this computer: it is not synced, indexed for search, exported, or available to the CLI and MCP interfaces.

Choose how long dictations are kept: 1 day, 7 days (the default), 30 days or forever. Older dictations and their audio are deleted when Librelog starts, when you change this, and every hour. **Off** stops saving new dictations; dictations already saved stay until you delete them or choose **Clear all**.

Turn on **Keep dictation audio** to also save each dictation's recording, so you can choose **Transcribe again** with the selected transcription model or an on-device model installed on this computer. The text it replaces is kept as one of up to five earlier versions, and **Restore** switches back to it. Audio is deleted with its dictation. Turning the switch off stops saving audio; audio already saved stays until its dictation is deleted or expires.
