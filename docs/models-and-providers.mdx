Librelog does not hide one model behind the whole app. It keeps two independent selections:

- **Transcription** turns meeting audio into text.
- **Intelligence** turns text into summaries, titles, and chat responses.

Open **Settings → Transcription** and **Settings → Intelligence** to see the selection active on your computer. Changing one selection does not change the other. Each selection uses either a model that runs on your computer or a provider you configure with your own credentials. Librelog has no hosted model of its own.

## How a meeting moves through AI

| Stage | Input | Output | Controlled by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capture | Microphone and computer audio | A local recording | **Settings → Meetings** |
| Transcription | Meeting audio | Live or after-recording transcript | **Settings → Transcription** |
| Intelligence | Transcript, memo, meeting details, and your instructions | Summary, title, or chat response | **Settings → Intelligence** |

Recording, editing, and local storage do not require an AI model. A hosted Transcription provider receives audio. A hosted Intelligence provider receives the text and other context needed for that request.

## On-device models

Librelog offers four on-device Transcription providers. Settings lists a provider only on the platforms where its engine runs, and shows **Not available on this computer** when your build or system cannot run it. Models download directly from Hugging Face when you choose **Download**; none are bundled with the app.

| Provider | Model | When it runs | Requirements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soniqo | Parakeet Streaming | During the meeting | Apple Silicon; live transcription for 25 European languages |
| Soniqo | Parakeet Batch | After recording | Apple Silicon; speaker-labeled transcription for 25 European languages. Prefer this for hour-long calls. |
| Apple Speech | Apple Speech | During the meeting | macOS 26 with a supported language added in System Settings. Live transcription uses one language at a time from German, English, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Cantonese, or Chinese. |
| Local models | A downloadable model from the [transcribe.cpp catalog](#local-models) | After recording | Apple Silicon Mac, Windows x86_64, or Linux x86_64 or ARM64. On Windows and Linux, [build Librelog from source](/desktop-installation#build-from-source). An x86_64 processor needs AVX2. |
| BYO-model | A whisper.cpp `.bin` or GGUF `.gguf` file you select | After recording | The same platforms as Local models. Choose **Choose a .bin or .gguf model** to pick the file. |

Parakeet Batch does not always provide word-level audio timestamps. When it returns text without a usable speech alignment, Librelog keeps the individual words editable and groups the display by audio channel and processing chunk. The order between channels within a chunk is uncertain, and those words cannot be used for precise audio seeking. Speech-aligned words retain their timing.

You can also keep audio on your network by pointing **Custom**, or Deepgram's **Advanced** base URL, at a Deepgram-compatible server on this computer or your local network.

<Note>
  Compatibility code still recognizes some older local model IDs, but they are not offered as new choices. The table above is the current user-facing model list.
</Note>

For local summaries and chat, download one of the [Built-in models](#built-in-models) on an Apple Silicon Mac, or connect **LM Studio**, **Ollama**, or **Unsloth** and choose any compatible model they serve. Librelog does not silently substitute another model. **Apple Intelligence** is also available as an experimental, text-only Intelligence provider on eligible Macs running macOS 26 or later.

If a selected local model or server is unavailable, Librelog reports a connection error instead of sending the request to another provider.

### Local models

**Local models** run speech models with [transcribe.cpp](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp) on your computer. Each model is downloaded on request from its publisher's Hugging Face repository, pinned to one revision and checked against its size and SHA-256, under its own license; Librelog does not redistribute the weights. Each model in Settings shows its download size, how many languages it covers, whether it gives word timing, a link to its license and to the repository it comes from, and any attribution the license asks for.

| Model | Download | Languages | Timing | License |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whisper large-v3-turbo | about 845 MB | About 100, with language detection | Segment | MIT |
| Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 | about 705 MB | 25 European languages | Word | CC BY 4.0 |
| Parakeet Ultra | about 706 MB | 25 European languages | Word | CC BY 4.0, with attribution |
| Qwen3-ASR 0.6B | about 811 MB | 30, with language detection | None | Apache 2.0 |
| Cohere Transcribe | about 2.2 GB | 14 | None | Apache 2.0 |
| GigaAM v3 | about 261 MB | Russian | Word | MIT |
| NB-Whisper Medium | about 514 MB | Norwegian | Segment | Apache 2.0 |
| ivrit.ai Whisper large-v3-turbo | about 845 MB | Hebrew | Segment | Apache 2.0 |
| Nemotron 3.5 ASR | about 716 MB | 27, with language detection | Word | OpenMDW 1.1 |

SenseVoice Small and Parakeet Unified English, which transcribe.cpp also runs, are not offered because their licenses restrict how they may be used.

- **Best for your languages** marks the model Librelog recommends for your main and spoken languages, and the list puts the next choices after it. For English and Portuguese, Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 comes first and Whisper large-v3-turbo second; when two models are about as accurate, the one with word timing comes first. A model that cannot transcribe one of your languages is never recommended.
- **Timing** affects speaker labels. Word timing places each word where it was spoken. With segment timing or none, word positions are estimated, so speaker separation after a meeting is less precise.
- Local models work after recording. During a meeting, Librelog transcribes the recording a minute at a time; when you stop, it transcribes the whole recording again if the recording is kept. They do not show live captions, and dictation shows no live preview while you speak.

#### Speech engine

On a computer with a supported GPU, the **Local models** and **BYO-model** pages show **Speech engine**:

- **Auto** uses a GPU that works and falls back to the CPU.
- **GPU** uses the GPU, even one set aside after a failure.
- **CPU** never uses the GPU.

The page lists the devices Librelog found. If a GPU fails to load or run a model, Librelog sets it aside and Auto uses the CPU; the page names that GPU, and **Try GPU again** clears the record. Changing the engine reloads the selected model.

Local models use Metal on Apple Silicon and Vulkan on Windows and Linux. The project has not yet exercised these GPU paths on real hardware, so treat GPU acceleration as untested. If transcription fails or stops on a GPU, choose **CPU**.

### Built-in models

**Built-in models** run language models for summaries, titles, and chat with [llama.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp), which ships inside the Librelog release for Apple Silicon Macs. Each model is one Q4_K_M GGUF file downloaded on request from its publisher's own Hugging Face repository, pinned to one revision and checked against its size and SHA-256. Settings shows each model's download size, license, source, and whether it fits your Mac.

| Model | Download | Most context in Librelog | License |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qwen3 4B | about 2.3 GB | 40,960 tokens | Apache 2.0 |
| Qwen3 8B | about 4.7 GB | 40,960 tokens | Apache 2.0 |
| Granite 4.0 Micro | about 2.0 GB | 65,536 tokens | Apache 2.0 |
| Ministral 3 8B | about 4.8 GB | 65,536 tokens | Apache 2.0 |
| Ministral 3 3B | about 2.0 GB | 65,536 tokens | Apache 2.0 |

- **Memory.** Librelog estimates each model's memory from its file and the context it needs, and loads it with the longest of 8,192, 16,384, 32,768, and 65,536 tokens, or the model's own limit if lower, that stays within half of your Mac's memory. It never reserves more than 65,536 tokens, however much memory is free. **Tight** means only the shortest context, 8,192 tokens, fits, using up to 65% of memory. **Too large for this Mac** models cannot be selected. On a Mac with 8 GB of memory or less, a model does not start while a meeting is being recorded or its recording transcribed; it starts once that is done. A model that is already running keeps serving.
- **Long meetings.** Librelog sends the whole transcript in one request. Each model shows about how many minutes of meeting it can read at once on your Mac. A meeting that leaves too little room for the summary fails with **This meeting is too long for this model**, before the model spends time on it; choose a model that reads more, or a hosted provider, for it. A summary that runs out of room while it is being written fails the same way and is not saved.
- **Thinking.** Built-in models answer without a separate reasoning step, so there is no reasoning-effort setting for them.
- **Lifecycle.** The model loads with the first request and unloads after five idle minutes. It listens only on `127.0.0.1` with a key that changes every time it starts, and Librelog stops it when you quit.

### Speakers after a live on-device meeting

During the meeting, Parakeet Streaming and Apple Speech label words only by where they came from: your microphone, or your computer's audio. Everyone you hear through the computer shares one speaker until the meeting ends. When you stop, Librelog transcribes the saved recording again on this Mac and separates the voices on the computer-audio channel:

- If **Parakeet Batch** is downloaded and supports the meeting's language, Librelog uses it. With Apple Speech and no usable Parakeet Batch, Apple Speech transcribes the recording again.
- Parakeet Streaming cannot separate speakers by itself. Librelog never downloads Parakeet Batch for this on its own; it shows a hint next to the transcript instead.
- The recording must be kept. With **Audio file retention** set to **Don't save**, voices are not separated.
- It runs when the meeting lists no participants besides you, or two or more. With exactly one other participant, the far end stays one speaker named after that person. Without listed participants, a recording with no speech from the computer's audio, such as a memo, is skipped.
- The transcript shows **Separating speakers...** while it runs, and the summary is written from the result. If you stop it, start another recording, or it fails, the live transcript stays as it was.
- Your microphone channel is only split for an in-person meeting that has no computer audio and lists two or more other participants.

The new transcript replaces the live one for that recording. Speakers you named during the meeting keep their names where the words line up. Turn this off under **Settings → Meetings → Separate speakers after meetings** if you prefer not to spend the extra minutes and processing after each meeting.

## Other model-backed features

Not every AI-adjacent feature is controlled by AI Settings:

| Feature | Implementation | Where it runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting search | Tantivy full-text index, not an LLM or embedding search | On your computer |
| Speaker voiceprints | `wespeaker-embedding-onnx-1` voice embeddings | On your computer |
| Audio cleanup and speech detection | Bundled ONNX models | On your computer |

## Your own provider

When you configure a provider with your own API key:

- Librelog sends requests directly from the desktop app to the configured base URL.
- The provider and exact model shown in Settings handle the request.
- Librelog stores the API key in your operating system's secure credential store, not in the app database. Keys stay on that computer.
- The provider's pricing, retention, and training policies apply.

Librelog supports dedicated provider integrations and OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Intelligence model lists are loaded from the provider when its API supports discovery, so **Settings → Intelligence** is the current source of truth for available model IDs.

You can also connect a subscription for Intelligence instead of an API key: **Claude**, **ChatGPT**, **Grok**, **GitHub Copilot**, or **Kimi Code**. Open **Settings → Intelligence → Configure Providers** and use **Connect**. Librelog signs in with that provider directly.

Recent bring-your-own Intelligence providers include **Venice**, configured with a Venice API key, and **Meta Muse** (`muse-spark-1.3` and the other `muse-spark` models), configured with a Meta Model API key.

Recent bring-your-own Transcription models in Settings include:

- **Google Gemini 3.5 Transcribe** (`gemini-3.5-transcribe-live` during the meeting, `gemini-3.5-transcribe` after recording)
- **AssemblyAI Universal 3.5 Pro** (`universal-3-5-pro-realtime` during the meeting, `universal-3-5-pro` after recording)
- **Smallest AI Pulse** (`pulse`) and **Pulse Pro** (`pulse-pro`) for live and after-recording transcription
- **Meta Muse Voice Transcribe** (`muse-voice-transcribe-1.0`) for live and after-recording transcription with speaker labels
- **Nari Labs** for live transcription with a Nari API key. Saved beta model IDs that ended in `:free` move to the current models.
- **Wispr Flow** for transcription and dictation with your own API key. Its after-recording adapter accepts at most six minutes per request; choose another provider for longer files.
- **Inworld**, **Gradium**, **Modulate**, and **Alebex** for live transcription with your own provider credentials.
- **NVIDIA Speech NIM** for live transcription through your own deployed NIM server. NVIDIA's hosted gRPC endpoint is not supported.
- **Amazon Bedrock** for after-recording transcription through an OpenAI-compatible gateway exposing `/audio/transcriptions`. Native Bedrock endpoints are not supported.

Librelog transcribes **AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro** (`universal-3-5-pro`) recordings once after you stop. Older Universal-3 Pro selections now use Universal-3.5 Pro. Processing the whole meeting preserves speaker labels across the recording instead of restarting them for each minute.

The new live-only providers keep credentials unverified until transcription starts. If live transcription fails, keep the recording and retry with a provider that supports after-recording transcription.

Settings may still show older model IDs for compatibility. Prefer the current Gemini 3.5, Universal 3.5, Soniox 5, Smallest AI Pulse, and GPT Transcribe models when you configure a provider yourself.

## Choose a setup

| Goal | Transcription | Intelligence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep meeting content on your computer | Select Soniqo, Apple Speech, Local models, or BYO-model when available, or a Deepgram-compatible local server | Use Built-in models, LM Studio, Ollama, Unsloth, or eligible Apple Intelligence |
| Avoid per-minute transcription bills | Use on-device models or a local Deepgram-compatible server | Use a local Intelligence provider or a connected subscription |
| Pin exact model IDs | Configure your own provider | Configure your own provider |
