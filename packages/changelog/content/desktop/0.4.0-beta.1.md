## Built-in models

- **Summaries without another app.** Choose **Built-in models** under **On this computer** in **Settings → Intelligence**, and Librelog writes summaries, titles and chat answers with a model that runs on your Mac. You no longer need Ollama or LM Studio for that.
- **Five models, downloaded when you ask:** Qwen3 4B (~2.3 GB, the default), Qwen3 8B (~4.7 GB), Granite 4.0 Micro (~2 GB), Ministral 3 8B (~4.8 GB) and Ministral 3 3B (~2 GB). All are under the Apache 2.0 license, and each shows its license and a link to its source. **Best for this Mac** marks the one Librelog recommends for your Mac's memory.
- **Checked downloads.** Each model comes from its publisher's Hugging Face repository and is checked against its expected size and SHA-256. Librelog checks for free disk space first, and a download that breaks off resumes where it stopped.
- **Fit and meeting length.** Each model shows whether it **Fits** your Mac's memory, is **Tight**, or is **Too large for this Mac**, and about how many minutes of meeting it reads at once. Built-in models read the whole meeting in one go: a meeting too long for the model fails with **This meeting is too long for this model** instead of saving a summary that was cut off. Use a model that reads more, or a cloud model, for those meetings.
- **Running only when needed.** The model loads with the first summary or chat, which can take a minute, and unloads after about five idle minutes to free memory. **Model server** shows whether it is running, with **Stop** to unload it now. Only your Mac can reach it, and it stops when you quit Librelog.
- On a Mac with 8 GB of memory or less, a built-in model doesn't start while a meeting is being recorded or transcribed, so transcription keeps the memory it needs. It starts once that is done.

## Local models

- **Open speech models on your Mac.** **Local models**, a new provider under **On this computer** in **Settings → Transcription**, downloads a speech model when you ask and runs it with transcribe.cpp, so your audio never leaves your Mac.
- **Nine models:** Whisper large-v3-turbo (~845 MB, about 100 languages), Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 (~705 MB) and Parakeet Ultra (~706 MB) for 25 European languages, Qwen3-ASR 0.6B (~811 MB, 30 languages), Cohere Transcribe (~2.2 GB, 14 languages), Nemotron 3.5 ASR (~716 MB, 27 languages), GigaAM v3 (~261 MB, Russian), NB-Whisper Medium (~514 MB, Norwegian) and ivrit.ai Whisper large-v3-turbo (~845 MB, Hebrew).
- Each model shows its size, how many languages it covers, its timing, its license and source, and any attribution its license asks for. Downloads come from Hugging Face, pinned to one version and checked against their size and SHA-256.
- **Best for your languages** marks the model that suits your main and spoken languages. For English and Portuguese that is Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, then Whisper large-v3-turbo. A model that can't transcribe one of your languages is never recommended.
- **After recording.** During a meeting, text appears a minute at a time, and when you stop, the whole recording is transcribed again if it is kept. There are no live captions, and dictation shows no preview while you speak. When a local model is your transcription model, dictation uses it too, and **Transcribe again** in **Settings → Dictation history** can use a local model you downloaded.
- **Timing and speakers.** Parakeet, Nemotron and GigaAM give the time of each word. The Whisper models give times per segment, and Qwen3-ASR and Cohere Transcribe give none, so speaker separation after a meeting is less precise with them.
- **Speech engine** on the **Local models** and **BYO-model** pages chooses **Auto**, **GPU** or **CPU**. Auto uses the GPU and falls back to the CPU when the GPU fails; **Try GPU again** gives it another chance.
- **BYO-model** now takes a GGUF `.gguf` file as well as a whisper.cpp `.bin`: choose **Choose a .bin or .gguf model**.

## Your whisper.cpp models

- whisper.cpp `.bin` models, including one you chose with **BYO-model**, now run with transcribe.cpp instead of whisper.cpp. This path is new: if a `.bin` that worked before no longer loads, or transcribes worse than it did, choose **Help → Report Bug** and tell us which file.

## Speakers after meetings

- **Everyone on a call, told apart.** With Parakeet Streaming or Apple Speech, everyone you hear through your Mac shares one speaker while the meeting runs. When you stop, Librelog now transcribes the kept recording again on your Mac and separates those voices. The transcript shows **Separating speakers...** while it works, and the summary is written from the result. If you stop it, start another recording, or it fails, the live transcript stays as it was.
- **No participant list needed.** It works without listing anyone. With exactly one other participant listed, the far end stays one speaker named after that person, as before.
- **Name a voice once.** Select a speaker label, choose the person and keep **Apply to all** on. With **Remember speakers** on, as it is by default, later meetings name that voice for you, even when the person isn't listed as a participant.
- **What it needs.** The recording must be kept, so **Audio file retention** can't be **Don't save**. Librelog uses Parakeet Batch when it is downloaded and speaks the meeting's language; with Apple Speech and no usable Parakeet Batch, Apple Speech transcribes the recording again. Parakeet Streaming can't separate voices on its own, so without Parakeet Batch they stay one speaker, and a notice beside the transcript says Parakeet Batch is needed. Librelog never downloads it for you.
- It takes a few minutes after each meeting. Turn it off with **Separate speakers after meetings** under **Audio** in **Settings → Meetings**.

## Floating bar

- The floating bar is now off by default, including if you had it on before. Turn it on with **Show floating bar** in **Settings → Meetings**, or choose **Show floating bar** from the **•••** menu of the note you are recording.
- Click **×** on the bar, or choose **Hide floating bar** from the same menu, to hide it during a meeting. Recording continues, and the bar stays off until you turn it on again.
- Without the bar, the menu bar icon animates while Librelog listens, and the note you are recording is live in the sidebar. Stop with **Stop** in the note's header, the stop button next to the note in the sidebar, or **Stop** on the **Did your meeting end?** notification.
- Live captions on screen are part of the floating bar. With it off, follow the live text in the note's **Transcript**.

## Windows and Linux

- **Local models** and **BYO-model** also run in source builds for Windows x86_64 and Linux x86_64 or ARM64, on Vulkan or the CPU. There are still no prebuilt Windows or Linux packages.

## Known limitations

- Librelog is not notarized by Apple yet. The first launch needs **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
- After an update, macOS may ask again for microphone, screen and system audio recording, accessibility, or keychain access. Dictation into other apps needs accessibility access.
- Librelog needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later).
- Local models and speaker separation after meetings have been checked with automated tests and on a Linux computer without a GPU, but not yet on a Mac by the project. GPU acceleration for local models is untested too; if transcription fails or stops on the GPU, choose **CPU** under **Speech engine**.
- Built-in models have been checked with automated tests against a Linux build of the same model server, but not yet on a Mac by the project. Their memory figures and minutes of meeting are estimates, not measurements.
- Built-in models are not in the App Store version of Librelog.
- On a call with one other person and no participants listed, that person's voice can come out as two speakers. List the participant to avoid it, or name both speakers as the same person.
- SenseVoice Small and Parakeet Unified English are not offered as local models, because their licenses restrict how they may be used.
- On macOS, **Pause media** reaches only Music, Spotify and VLC. macOS can ask for each one only while it is running, so open a player before clicking **Allow…**; a player you haven't allowed keeps playing. To change your answer, open **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation**.
- **Mute** and **Lower audio** act on the default output device only.
