## Screenshots (experimental)

- **Settings → Screenshots** turns on a new way to capture your screen. It is off by default, and macOS asks for **Screen Recording** the first time.
- Press Option + Shift + 4 and drag to capture an area. The screen freezes while you choose, so open menus stay in the picture. Press Space to pick a window instead, and Escape to cancel.
- Press Option + Shift + 3 to capture the whole display under the pointer.
- After a capture, the picture is copied to your clipboard and saved to your **Downloads** folder, and a small card appears in the corner of the screen. You can change each of these, and choose another folder, in **Settings → Screenshots**.
- The card lets you copy, show the file in Finder, open the editor, or drag the file straight into another app. It leaves by itself after a few seconds.

## Mark up a screenshot

- Click a card, or choose **Edit** in **Recent captures**, to open the editor.
- Draw arrows, lines, rectangles, ellipses, freehand pen and highlighter strokes, add text and numbered counters, pixelate what should not be read, and crop.
- Pick from seven colors and three sizes, move and resize what you drew, and undo or redo any step.
- **Copy** puts the result on the clipboard, **Save** replaces the screenshot's file, and **Save As…** writes a copy somewhere else.

## Record your screen as a video or a GIF

- Press Option + Shift + 5 to record a video (MP4), or Option + Shift + 6 to record a GIF. Drag an area, press Space to pick a window, or press Return to record the whole screen.
- A thin outline shows what is being recorded and a small capsule shows the time, with buttons to stop and to discard. Neither appears in the recording. Press the shortcut again to stop.
- Videos can include the pointer, a 3-second countdown, your microphone and the Mac's sound. GIFs can be 480, 720 or 1080 pixels wide at 10, 15 or 20 frames per second.
- Recordings are copied, saved and shown on a card like screenshots.

## Use the Mac's own screenshot shortcuts

- **Replace the macOS screenshot shortcuts** in **Settings → Screenshots** turns off macOS's Command + Shift + 3, 4 and 5 and gives them to Librelog. Command + Shift + 6 records a GIF.
- Librelog remembers how the macOS shortcuts were set and puts them back when you turn this off, or when you turn Screenshots off.
- If macOS does not accept the change, Librelog shows how to turn the shortcuts off yourself in **System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Screenshots**.

## Set it up when you first open Librelog

- The welcome steps have a new optional step for screenshots and recordings, where you can turn them on, choose the shortcuts and replace the macOS ones. You can skip it.

## Annotate during a meeting

- You can now use **Annotate** while Librelog records a meeting. Your voice goes to both: the meeting's transcript and the annotation's narration.
- An annotation started during a meeting records the same microphone the meeting uses.
- If the meeting's microphone is muted, the annotation still hears you, and nothing you say reaches the meeting.
- If another recording is being transcribed on this Mac when an annotation finishes, the annotation waits for it instead of being written without your words.

## Known limitations

- Screenshots, recordings and the capture cards were written without a Mac to try them on. They have been built and tested by automated checks, but nobody has captured anything with them yet. If the screen stays frozen or dimmed, press Escape. If that does not help, quit Librelog from the menu bar, and tell us.
- Replacing the macOS shortcuts changes a macOS setting. It has not been tried on a Mac either. Turn it off before you uninstall Librelog, so macOS gets its shortcuts back.
- Recording your microphone and the Mac's sound in a video is experimental. A video with both has two separate audio tracks, and some players play only the first.
- Recording a window records the area the window covered when you picked it. It does not follow the window if you move it.
- Videos are at most 4096 × 2304 pixels and 30 minutes long. A long GIF lowers its frame rate to stay a reasonable size.
- Annotate during a meeting has not been tried in a real call. Use headphones: without them, the other people's voices reach your microphone and the annotation's narration.
- Screenshots and recordings work only in the macOS app downloaded from the website, on one display at a time, and one capture at a time.
- Librelog is not notarized by Apple yet. The first launch needs **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
- Librelog needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later).
